## Semantic consistency results

- **Product role:** AyuVahak remains a coordination platform, not an EHR/EMR, HIS, HIE replacement, generic ESB/integration engine, DHIS2 replacement, LMIS, national patient database, ambulance ERP, or general BI system. Each adjacent-system boundary is described concretely in the [scope](../product/scope-and-non-goals.md). If another system owns the same coordination lifecycle, a deployment may omit AyuVahak.
- **Authority:** AyuVahak owns only its coordination records/actions where selected as workflow owner. Clinical records, patient identity, registries, credentials, commodity stock, dispatch assignment, and public-health indicator definitions stay with category authorities. References, transient processing, bounded transfer context, operational state, and aggregates are distinguished in [data ownership](../architecture/data-ownership.md).
- **Domain language:** Referral, Transfer Request, Transfer, and Transport stay separate. Capability, Capacity, Availability, and Readiness stay separate. Recommendation is advisory; destination choice is an authorized human decision. Clinical Suitability Assessment, Operational Readiness Assessment, Acceptance, and Reservation remain distinct. Arrival does not imply Handover.
- **Decision authority:** Receiving authority is modeled as locally mapped functions, not globally fixed job titles. Authorization remains a deployment policy question and authentication is not patient identity or action authorization.
- **Interoperability:** Domain-owned ports/adapters are consistent across context, system, standards, and workflow views. Shared support is technical infrastructure, not a universal gateway or workflow owner. FHIR is a boundary contract, never the core domain schema. R4 is the initial version choice.
- **External infrastructure:** DHIS2 is an optional downstream aggregate target in the AyuVahak baseline and is not on the synchronous care transaction path. DHIS2 Tracker and OpenHIM overlap are acknowledged; a deployment selects one workflow owner. LMIS is outside initial workflow dependencies.
- **Trust and privacy:** Normal logs do not need PHI or clinical payloads. Patient context is minimum-necessary and case-scoped; attachments are reference-only in the baseline; raw external snapshots are transient; analytics are minimized and aggregate/de-identified by default. Authentication and local authorization are separate; machine identities are scoped. No home-grown cryptography or password store is specified.
- **Failure behavior:** Stale/missing data is unknown; external failure cannot fabricate availability, Acceptance, Reservation, dispatch, Arrival, or Handover. Duplicate/delayed facts preserve chronology. Acceptance remains committed after a publication failure, while outbox delivery remains recoverable. No distributed transaction is assumed.
- **Deployment and extraction:** The diagrams show one modular monolith, not independently deployed services. No mandatory Kafka, Kubernetes, service mesh, proprietary cloud, public test server, or always-on national infrastructure is required. Package/ownership rules and a later ArchUnit CI baseline are explicit.
- **Standards:** Versioned claims are tied to primary documentation and a retrieval date. FHIR resources are described as possible boundary representations; standard capabilities are not confused with AyuVahak's chosen lifecycle semantics. The system remains usable without FHIR, with other versions, and with local/manual adapters.
