# Volume III architecture and specification review

**Review date:** 2026-10-15

**Scope:** Volume III Phase 8–10 design and specifications against the frozen Volume I–II constitution, ADRs, context contracts, state machines, data ownership, security/privacy constraints, and official standards research. Documentation-only review; no implementation was performed.
**Review status:** Final independent design reviews complete; all findings dispositioned. This review predates implementation and does not replace the separate phase implementation reviews and acceptance records.

## Review result

**Result:** PASS. Independent standards/interoperability and security/transport reviewers confirmed the remediations. No issue required changing the approved Volume I–II ownership model. At the time of this design review, Volume III implementation had not started and remained separately gated.

## Review method

The review challenged the design for: FHIR representation leaking into domain aggregates; central integration/gateway ownership; misuse of mCSD, IPS, SMART, or schedule/slot semantics; patient-summary retention and recipient authorization; unsafe endpoint selection/SSRF; partner credential and replay handling; external delivery being mistaken for a business outcome; Transport and Transfer ownership being collapsed; EMS standard/provider assumptions; and dependence on public systems for tests.

Standards claims were checked against the official sources and recorded in [Volume III standards research](../research/volume-3-standards-research.md), with publication versions and retrieval date. Standards facts are separated from AyuVahak decisions.

Independent reviewers identified the findings and re-reviewed the relevant remediations. The official mCSD source resolved V3-08 as a factual clarification. Both final reviewers confirmed the Phase 10 contracts for destination change, request eligibility, Capacity hold release/recovery, and outcome naming. The data-ownership record now distinguishes the old-hold release after acceptance from the proposed-hold release after rejection. The state machine explicitly allows factual movement/arrival evidence to continue while a reroute outcome is pending.

## Semantic grill conclusions

- FHIR resources remain boundary representations. Owner contexts retain their internal semantics, mappings, external identifier associations, and reconciliation rules.
- Shared Phase 9 capability enforces partner transport/trust controls only; it does not become a universal gateway, mapping owner, business retry owner, or cross-context writer.
- mCSD feeds/query results are Directory source evidence. Partial or absent results do not imply deletion; update/feed options are separate and optional.
- IPS is a clinical summary representation, not a patient chart, identity authority, consent proof, or completeness guarantee. Referral owns disclosure policy and source retrieval; Transfer establishes selected-recipient participation first.
- OAuth/SMART authenticates a partner/client within its declared scope; AyuVahak grants and owner policy still authorize domain actions.
- Transport is a separate owner for movement coordination; Transfer remains authoritative for acceptance, reservation relationship, arrival, handover, completion, and destination change. Manual mode is viable without EMS.
- All Phase 8–10 acceptance tests use synthetic data and local infrastructure. No public healthcare target is required.

## Scope and safety review

The proposed design contains no HIE replacement, generic integration platform, clinical record warehouse, MPI, Capacity-to-Slot shortcut, ambulance ERP/CAD, continuous GPS system, or mandatory broker/cloud/service. No requirements depend on permanent connectivity. Failure outcomes remain explicit and owner state does not become positive because an external call timed out or returned a syntactically valid payload.
