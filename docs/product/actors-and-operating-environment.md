# Actors and Operating Environments

AyuVahak coordinates among actors with different authority. Access is purpose- and case-scoped; a relationship with a facility or system does not grant access to every patient record. “Must not automatically receive” describes the default boundary and can change only under an authorized, documented purpose and local policy. Receiving-side authority functions are stable domain concepts; local role titles and organizational assignments vary by deployment.

## Human actors

| Actor | Goals and responsibilities | Required information | Must not automatically receive | Critical interactions | Trust assumptions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Patient or authorized caregiver | Receive understandable coordination information, provide or confirm relevant details where possible, and participate in care decisions according to capacity and local law. | Explanation of the referral, planned destination and transport, expected sharing of minimum context, and a way to ask questions or correct identity details. | Other patients' information, internal security/audit details, or staff-only operational information unrelated to their care. | May provide/confirm information or consent where applicable; receives authorized status and participates in receiving/handover decisions through the care team. | Identity and representative authority are verified as appropriate; ability to consent varies, and emergency/legal rules are deployment-specific. They are not assumed to have an AyuVahak account. |
| Referring clinician | Assess the patient, state the Care Requirement, review candidates, select a destination, and remain clinically accountable. | Minimum relevant patient context; candidate capability and capacity evidence with timestamps; Transfer Request and Transfer status. | Unrelated patient records, another facility's broader census, or hidden/unsupported recommendation inputs. | Creates or updates referral; reviews and acts on recommendations; receives response and transfer updates. | Authenticated and authorized clinician; clinical judgement and source facts remain their responsibility. |
| Referral operator | Perform coordination delegated by the referring team and keep the case current. | Referral identifiers, minimum patient logistics, clinician-supplied requirements, candidate/contact and status information. | Authority to invent clinical requirements or view full records without a role and purpose. | Enters referral, issues/monitors requests, contacts facilities, records out-of-band updates. | Trained and scoped to a facility/team; delegated facts are attributable to their source. |
| Receiving operational-readiness authority (often a coordinator) | Determine whether the facility can operationally receive this case now and manage any capacity commitment. | Request, care requirement at the minimum operational level, ETA, current capacity evidence. | Full longitudinal chart, unrelated cases, or patient data not needed for the operational decision. | Records an Operational Readiness Assessment; coordinates with clinical-suitability authority; confirms/releases Reservation when separately authorized; reports lost capacity. | Local facility policy grants the operational authority; the title may be transfer-center nurse, bed manager, referral-desk operator, command-center role, team, or another accountable assignment. |
| Receiving clinical-suitability authority (often a clinician) | Assess whether the facility can meet this case's Care Requirement and retain clinical decision accountability. | Relevant minimum Patient Context, Care Requirement, clinical urgency, and expected arrival. | Data about other patients, broad facility staffing/census, or unrelated administrative records. | Records a Clinical Suitability Assessment or rejection; consults operational-readiness authority; receives deterioration updates; may acknowledge Handover if locally authorized. | Local clinical governance assigns the authority; the title may be emergency physician, specialty registrar, medical officer, clinical team, or a governed process with an accountable owner. |
| Facility Acceptance Authority | Record the facility's willingness to proceed after the separate clinical and operational assessments are positive. | Transfer Request, both attributed assessments, stated conditions, and relevant expiry. | Unrelated patient records or operational information outside the authority's scope. | Records Acceptance or a facility-level decline; coordinates the separate assessments; does not create a Reservation unless separately authorized. | Named by local policy. It may be the same person/team as an assessment authority only when local policy explicitly permits it; the distinct assessment facts remain visible. |
| Hospital administrator | Govern local services, workforce, operational policies, and facility participation. | Facility/service/capability profile, aggregated capacity and workflow measures, governance exceptions. | Routine patient names, full summaries, or case narrative by virtue of an administrative title alone. | Maintains operating profile; delegates roles; reviews aggregate performance and exceptions. | Authorized organizational administrator; operational data they attest may become stale. |
| EMS/transport dispatcher | Decide whether and how to assign a transport resource and control dispatch. | Pickup/drop-off, requested mode/priority, timing, minimum safety/logistics details needed for assignment. | Full clinical record, unrelated referral candidates, or data outside transport purpose. | Receives transport request; assigns/declines; sends dispatch/status updates; coordinates changes. | Dispatcher/source system governs dispatch truth; AyuVahak status is a coordination copy. |
| Ambulance/transport crew | Safely collect and move the patient and communicate actual status. | Identity confirmation, pickup/drop-off, essential precautions, authorized contact and handoff details. | Full longitudinal record, unrelated case data, or ranking rationale not needed for transport. | Receives task; reports pickup/departure/arrival or inability; hands patient to receiving team. | Crew role is verified by dispatch/employer; device connectivity and message time may be imperfect. |
| District/regional coordinator | Resolve cross-facility bottlenecks, support escalation, and monitor network capacity. | Cross-facility directory, freshness-qualified operational view, escalation contacts, minimized case status where authorized. | Patient summaries or identifiable details by default; access requires explicit role and purpose. | Escalates with facilities; assists when candidates fail; reviews aggregate flow. | Health authority controls scope; regional view may lag local sources. |
| Public-health analyst | Understand coordination patterns and approved public-health measures. | Aggregated, governed indicators and provenance/quality notes. | Patient identifiers, case narrative, or live operational referral stream. | Receives approved aggregate exports; interprets data limitations with system owners. | Authorized for aggregate use; analytics confers no clinical authority. |
| System operator | Maintain platform availability, security, backup, and incident response. | Service health, delivery/error metadata, security events, minimized diagnostic context. | Patient summaries or case data by default; break-glass access must be controlled and audited. | Operates deployment; handles technical incidents; escalates privacy/security issues. | Trusted for technical operations, not automatically for clinical access. |
| System administrator | Configure organizations, facilities, roles, policies, and integration endpoints. | Configuration metadata, organization relationships, role assignments, integration health. | Patient content or clinical case details by virtue of configuration authority. | Onboards organizations/users; configures policy; disables access; reviews audit. | Privileged, individually attributable, least-privileged, and periodically reviewed. |
| Auditor | Verify access, decisions, provenance, and compliance with approved policy. | Actor, purpose, timestamps, source, state transitions, and authorized case references. | Patient content unless a specific audit question requires approved, time-limited access. | Queries audit records; requests an authorized evidence view; documents findings. | Independent and scoped; audit access itself is recorded. |

## Machine and organization actors

| Actor | Goal and responsibility | Required information | Must not automatically receive | Critical interactions | Trust assumptions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| EHR/HIS | Authoritative clinical record and local encounter/referral context; may supply a scoped patient reference or summary. | Scoped exchange request and authorized status/result for its own patient/referral. | Other facilities' cases, full coordination analytics, or unrelated patient information. | Supplies patient context; receives permitted status; remains authoritative for clinical record. | Correct for its own record, not guaranteed current or semantically identical to another source. |
| HIE/interoperability layer, including OpenHIM | Route, mediate, or transform authorized exchanges between participants. | Explicit endpoint, message, routing metadata, and only payload required for the exchange. | Credentials or payloads outside the transaction; no independent patient or capacity authority. | Carries/mediates messages between adapters and partners. | Trusted for configured routing; delivery does not prove source truth or recipient action. |
| Facility registry | Publish organization/facility/location/service identifiers and relationships. | Registry query/update in its domain; no referral payload is needed. | Patient context or coordination case events. | Supplies identifiers/details; AyuVahak preserves source and refresh time. | Authority is scoped to registry data; completeness and freshness are checked. |
| Provider registry | Publish practitioner identifier, registration, or credential attributes. | Practitioner identifiers and limited validation queries. | Patient cases or clinical summaries. | Resolves provider references when required. | Authority is limited to maintained attributes; not a source of application permissions. |
| MPI/client registry | Link patient identifiers across clinical systems when legitimately queried. | Minimum identity attributes needed for an authorized match. | Full case narrative, destination options, or unrelated clinical history. | Returns source patient reference/link; AyuVahak does not copy or invent a merge. | Match results are treated according to registry guarantees; ambiguity is surfaced. |
| Identity provider (IdP) | Authenticate workforce or partner identities and provide configured identity claims. | Login protocol data and minimal client identity. | Patient data or referral content as a default login claim. | Authenticates user; supplies subject/group claims; accepts revocation. | Identity proof is trusted within issuer/configuration; app authorization remains separate. |
| DHIS2 | Maintain public-health indicators and program information. | Approved aggregate coordination measures and metadata. | Patient identifiers, direct case feed, or operational acceptance/transfer commands. | Receives one-way governed aggregate export where enabled. | Authority is scoped to configured indicators; export failure cannot block live coordination. |
| LMIS/OpenLMIS | Maintain medicine and commodity supply-chain information. | No routine referral data; only future, explicit, purpose-limited exchange if justified. | Patient summaries, referral state, or clinical events. | Remains the stock authority; initial matching does not infer capacity from inventory. | Authoritative for stock domain, not clinical readiness or bed capacity. |
| Routing provider | Estimate route or travel information between locations. | Origin/destination coordinates and travel mode; patient identity is unnecessary. | Patient identity, summary, care requirement, or named case. | Supplies an attributed route estimate as one ranking input when available. | Estimate may be stale/inaccurate; it is not a dispatch instruction or safety guarantee. |
| Notification gateway | Deliver an authorized alert and report delivery status. | Recipient, minimal message, expiry, and correlation reference. | Clinical summary or sensitive identifiers unless the channel is approved for that purpose. | Delivers status/request alerts; failures prompt human follow-up. | Delivery receipt does not prove a human read or acted. |

## Operating environments

| Environment | Architectural and operating implications |
| --- | --- |
| Single hospital | May coordinate departments or a small partner set. Existing HIS/EHR stays authoritative; local configuration and manual capacity entry must be possible. |
| Hospital network | Facilities may share directory, identity, or governance, but each remains accountable for its capacity and acceptance. Cross-site authorization and facility-scoped views are necessary. |
| District deployment | Organizations may have uneven systems and staffing. A district coordinator needs a freshness-aware view and escalation path, not blanket access to patient records. |
| Regional deployment | Services, distances, and jurisdictions are more heterogeneous. Policies and source authority vary by organization; ranking must explain constraints and data quality. |
| National or federated deployment | Independent deployments may retain local authority and data. Federation exchanges minimum discovery/coordination facts and references rather than centralizing clinical records. Jurisdiction, consent, and retention are configured by accountable authorities. |
| Intermittent connectivity | A network partition is a normal degraded mode. Pending actions remain visibly pending; stale capacity is not available; high-risk decisions use phone/radio or local policy and are reconciled with timestamps and attribution. Offline conflict semantics are a future validated capability, not assumed. |
| Legacy systems | Do not assume FHIR, stable identifiers, or reliable APIs. Begin with accountable manual entry or controlled exchange, preserve provenance, and make reconciliation visible. |
| Manually maintained directory/capacity | Record who asserted a fact, when it was observed, its validity period, and how to contact the source. Manual data is useful but not equivalent to a live feed. |
| Mature FHIR-based environment | FHIR R4 reduces transport/structure variation but does not settle local profiles, authority, consent, semantics, freshness, or workflow. Partner conformance and versioned mappings remain necessary. |

## Mapping local roles to decision functions

Each deployment records which person, team, or governed process may perform the Clinical Suitability Assessment, Operational Readiness Assessment, facility Acceptance, Reservation, and Handover acknowledgement. The mapping is part of local authorization policy, not a global AyuVahak job-title list. Authorization must be scoped by organization/facility, action, case relationship, and purpose. If a local automated policy is allowed to make an assessment, its authority, version, accountable owner, evidence inputs, and escalation path must be explicit; it cannot be treated as clinical judgement merely because it is automated.
