# Volume V retention and deletion governance

**Status:** Frozen Volume V design input for Phase 13; deployment schedules and legal authority remain deployment-specific. This document authorizes no retention implementation or deletion.

## Purpose and invariant

This matrix makes retention ownership explicit without inventing a jurisdiction-wide duration. Each owner controls its own data lifecycle and must implement a bounded schedule only after the accountable deployment authority identifies purpose, applicable obligations, and a retention period. A retention job may not erase evidence needed for a live business operation, unresolved outcome, replay/deduplication window, audit integrity, or Analytics disclosure-history continuity.

Retention expiration is not itself proof that data may be deleted. Legal hold, active-case need, safety investigation, unresolved reconciliation, or a frozen owner invariant can extend or block disposal. Such a hold must be purpose-bound, access-restricted, attributable, periodically reviewed, and removable by an authorized owner. No blanket indefinite retention is implied. No blanket right-to-delete or universal anonymization behavior is inferred either.

When a record is eligible for disposal, its owner selects the least-retentive safe action: purge payload/content; minimize or pseudonymize direct identifiers while preserving a necessary reference; expire a projection; or retain a restricted integrity/audit tombstone. The owner records what was disposed and why without copying the disposed sensitive content into audit. Backup copies expire according to their separate schedule; a restore must reapply recorded retention/deletion controls before serving traffic.

## Owner-by-owner design matrix

Durations are deliberately unset. Before a deployment uses real operational data, its data controller/authority and records/privacy owner must select schedules and holds by category. The implementation must make the schedule owner/configuration and deletion result observable without logging the data itself.

| Owner and category | Retention purpose and class | Expiry, purge, or minimization behavior | Evidence that must remain coherent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Platform identity, authentication subject, grants and revocations | Minimal account/subject references, current grants, and attributable grant history support access control and investigation. Credentials, tokens, and private keys are never retained in application business storage. | Revoke access immediately when authorized; remove or pseudonymize inactive account attributes when no longer needed. Retain only the minimum subject/grant history required by the configured accountability period. Secret material remains with the configured provider and follows its rotation/revocation lifecycle. | Audit attribution may use a restricted pseudonymous actor reference. Grant revocation and its effective time cannot be erased while retained actions depend on it. No credential archive is created.
| Platform audit | Restricted accountability evidence for protected actions, denials, configuration changes, and owner outcomes; not a log or payload archive. | Append-only/tamper-evident during the configured retention period. Authorized disposal occurs only after the applicable schedule/hold and integrity policy permit it; use controlled partition/archive disposal or a minimal tombstone if required by the audit design. Never edit a retained event to satisfy an erasure request. | Chain/checkpoint integrity, actor/service reference, action, scope, purpose and sanitized outcome. No PHI, raw payload, secret, token, or clinical narrative is copied into audit.
| Platform command idempotency and consumer deduplication | Prevent repeated local effects during the supported client/event retry window. | Keep receipts at least through the configured maximum retry/replay horizon and any required audit interval. Purge after the horizon only when no unresolved operation depends on the receipt. Store fingerprints and result references, not request bodies. | Same-key/same-fingerprint replay remains safe during the promised horizon; changed fingerprint conflict behavior is not lost while the key is valid.
| Platform internal event/outbox delivery | Durable propagation and restart recovery; publication metadata belongs to Platform while business facts remain with their source owner. | Retain undelivered, leased, failed, or unhandled records until owner-defined recovery or explicit resolution. Delivered event and consumer receipts may be purged only after the documented replay/retention window and when no consumer recovery depends on them. Bound payload and do not archive clinical/external payloads. | Event ID, owner/aggregate sequence, delivery state and sanitized correlation remain sufficient for deduplication, ordering and recovery through the support window.
| Platform notification projection and cursor | Short-lived, scope-bound UI freshness hints. | Expire by the bounded replay policy; projection rebuild/reset changes cursor epoch and requires refresh. No permanent UI history. | Scope, epoch and sequence semantics remain safe; expired history cannot replay across scopes. Owner state remains authoritative.
| Directory | Source-attributed coordination projections for organizations, facilities, locations, services, endpoints and source provenance. | Retain while needed for current discovery and historical coordination. Source deactivation is an explicit state, not inferred from partial feed or deletion. Expire stale projections by source policy; purge obsolete projection content only when no retained coordination history requires its interpretation. | Source namespace/version, provenance and historical references used by prior decisions. Purge cannot relabel a remote source as locally owned or fabricate a deletion.
| Capacity | Current readiness projection, time-bounded observations, reservations, release and reconciliation history. | Current evidence expires to stale/unknown at its configured validity boundary. Historical observations follow the deployment operational schedule. A reservation remains until resolved/released/expired by its owner contract; unresolved reservation or release outcomes block unsafe disposal. | Reservation idempotency, source/evidence reference, policy/version and audit linkage until recovery and accountability horizons end. Expiry never changes stale/unknown to zero or ready.
| Referral | Coordination request/revision and, where authorized, opaque issuer-scoped PatientReference. No demographic, diagnosis, clinical narrative, IPS or attachment archive is part of the default model. | Closed-case restrictions, removal or pseudonymization are owner-mediated under the deployment case schedule and applicable authority. Remove/minimize PatientReference when no longer necessary, but only through an explicit workflow that preserves permitted case/audit integrity. Clinical payload remains transient and is discarded after the authorized exchange. | Revision and lifecycle chronology, stable non-identifying coordination references, and audit links needed to explain actions. Any pseudonymization must not create identity merges or restore access to a closed case.
| Matching | Recommendation snapshots, structured reasons, evidence versions and policy versions needed to explain prior recommendations. | Retain under a bounded decision-evidence schedule; no indefinite candidate/evidence archive. Purge or minimize snapshots after the schedule unless a case/audit hold requires them. | Indicator of evidence age, source/policy version and recommendation ordering for retained decisions; no PatientReference or full Directory/Capacity copies.
| Transfer | Transfer attempts, receiving assessments/acceptance, reservations references, movement/handover facts, corrections and reconciliation evidence. | Retain under the deployment case/recordkeeping schedule. Restrict or pseudonymize case linkage after closure when lawful and safe; do not delete prior attempts or rewrite chronology to make a later outcome appear original. Active or unknown operations prevent disposal of their recovery references. | Distinct owner facts, source/actor and occurred/recorded times, correction relationships, and idempotency/reconciliation references through their required windows.
| Transport | Minimal movement request, partner/manual assignment reference, status/ETA observations, cancellation/destination-change outcomes and provenance. No patient identifier, diagnosis, clinical summary, fleet inventory or GPS trail. | Retain only minimum operational history needed for coordination, reconciliation and audit under a bounded schedule. Expire ETA/current projections; purge obsolete status detail when safe. No default location history exists. | Stable request/operation identity and attributed outcome needed to reconcile unknown provider effects and linked Transfer evidence.
| Phase 9 secure exchange | Versioned partner trust/configuration metadata and technical attempts; business meaning remains with the owner context. Secret references only. | Revoke/suspend immediately; retain sanitized configuration/audit revisions under the trust-administration schedule. Owner-specific attempt metadata is removed only after retry/reconciliation and audit horizons end. Token/secret values are never retained in operation history. | Config version, endpoint identity and sanitized attempt class needed for attribution and unknown-outcome recovery. No generic business ledger or raw request/response archive.
| Phase 11 federation | Deployment/peer profiles, namespaced remote references, federated operations and message receipts; each business owner retains its own semantic state. | Peer revocation stops new use but does not erase history. Keep receipts through the peer replay window plus the configured audit period. An unknown remote outcome and its stable operation ID remain until reconciled or explicitly closed by an authorized owner. | Peer identity, operation ID/type/fingerprint, source sequence and sanitized outcome. No PatientReference or clinical payload.
| Phase 12 Analytics | Indicator/version definitions, bounded source snapshots, aggregate revisions, completeness, disclosure lineages/reservations, mappings and export/federation operations. Aggregates may remain sensitive. | Retain exact bounded inputs and source cutoffs only as long as needed for reproducibility, correction and disclosure review under the configured reporting policy. A released or potentially disclosed value and its lineage cannot be removed merely because a policy/mapping changes. Purge raw technical inputs and resolved export details only after correction, target-tuple, audit and disclosure windows permit. | Immutable revision links, policy/mapping version, stable disclosure lineage and prior/unknown release checkpoints. Unknown disclosure remains potentially disclosed. DHIS2 payloads/responses are not archived by default.
| Logs, metrics and traces | Operational/security diagnostics separate from audit. | Each surface has its own access, retention and disposal schedule. Logs/traces exclude PHI, prompts, secrets, tokens, raw partner payloads and high-cardinality case IDs. Metrics remain aggregate with bounded labels. | No diagnostic copy is needed to preserve business truth. Audit and owner records provide the accountable history.
| Backups and restore artifacts | Recovery of the authoritative database/configuration and declared RPO/RTO. | Encrypted, access-separated backups expire by the deployment schedule. Retention/deletion changes must be replayed or applied after restore before the restored environment serves traffic. Key material is recovered through the secret provider, not copied into database backups by convenience. | Backup manifest, database/WAL lineage, integrity evidence, recovery keys/config references and restore validation. A backup is not a business owner and does not override a deletion record.

## Required deployment schedule fields

Before live data is admitted, the accountable deployment authority must complete a schedule for every row above and identify:

1. data controller/authority, steward and operational owner;
2. purpose and data classification, including whether the record may contain an opaque case link or sensitive aggregate;
3. normal lifecycle trigger, minimum and maximum duration where appropriate, and event that starts the clock;
4. applicable legal/governance basis, holds, review owner and release authority;
5. expiry action: purge, projection expiry, minimization/pseudonymization, or restricted tombstone;
6. dependencies on audit, dedup/replay, unknown-outcome recovery, disclosure lineage, correction and restoration;
7. deletion propagation across replicas, exports, caches, observability, and backups, including what cannot be recalled from an already disclosed external aggregate;
8. verification method, completion evidence, failure alert and safe retry behavior.

An unset, contradictory or unverifiable schedule is not permission to retain forever or delete immediately. The owner fails closed for new collection/export where necessary, restricts access, and requires the deployment authority to resolve policy before real data is used.

## Phase 13 acceptance evidence

Phase 13 implementation must provide a retention register derived from this matrix and verify owner boundaries independently. Acceptance uses synthetic records and proves at least: expired notification projections do not replay; delivered outbox records are removed only after the replay horizon; an unresolved idempotency/federation/export operation is retained; a closed Referral's identifier restriction does not corrupt audit; expired Capacity evidence becomes stale/unknown; Analytics release history survives policy/mapping rollover and unresolved DHIS2 outcomes; restore reapplies completed retention actions; and held records are not purged. Every owner reports purge/minimization counts and failures without identifiers or payloads. The schedule and retention-action evidence remain configurable and deployment-owned; acceptance does not assert a legal duration.

## Frozen basis

This matrix elaborates the existing [data ownership](data-ownership.md), [Volume IV data ownership](volume-4-data-ownership.md), [Volume IV privacy matrix](volume-4-privacy-matrix.md), and owner specifications. It does not change any frozen owner, authorize a new schema, or define an individual's legal rights. A conflict is resolved in favor of the stricter existing privacy, safety, audit, and owner-recovery invariant until the accountable authority decides otherwise.
