# Standards and Interoperability Strategy

**Initial verification:** 2026-10-08. **Volume III targeted verification:** 2026-10-15. Publication status and implementation-library compatibility are version-sensitive and must be rechecked for a concrete deployment. Sources and exact retrieval date are in [Volume III standards research](../research/volume-3-standards-research.md).

## Architectural decision

Standards belong at integration seams. AyuVahak's internal domain language remains Referral, Care Requirement, Capacity Observation, Clinical Suitability Assessment, Operational Readiness Assessment, Acceptance, Reservation, Transfer, Transport, Arrival, and Handover. A domain context owns the port for an external need, the meaning of the exchanged fact or action, partner mapping, and business reconciliation. Its adapter translates the partner contract. Shared interoperability capabilities provide technical support; they do not route every interaction through a universal business gateway. See [bounded contexts](bounded-contexts.md) and [ADR 0010](../adr/0010-domain-owned-integration-ports.md).

Use HL7 FHIR R4 (4.0.1) as the initial interoperability baseline. Keep later R4B (4.3.0) and R5 (5.0.0) support in separately versioned boundary adapters. On 2026-10-15, HL7's publication directory listed R5 as the latest published release and R6 only as ballot builds/development snapshots; the R4-first choice is compatibility strategy, not a claim that R4 is newest. Confirm each partner's profiles, terminology, workflow, security, and supported FHIR version before exchange. No release-to-release conversion is assumed lossless.

## Verified standard and product boundaries

“Standard fact” describes what the published specification or product supports. “AyuVahak decision” describes how this platform chooses to use it. A fact does not itself require the decision.

## Volume III mapping and exchange constraints

Phase 8 uses context-owned R4 adapters rather than a FHIR business context. Directory maps organization/location/service discovery; Referral maps only approved transient patient context; Capacity maps only named operational-source evidence under Capacity policy. `ServiceRequest` and `Task` can represent parts of external request/workflow semantics but do not define a complete AyuVahak Referral or Transfer lifecycle. `Encounter` is not imported. `Endpoint` is untrusted discovery metadata until separately approved by Phase 9 partner configuration. Schedule/Slot is never operational bed/capacity evidence by default.

Phase 9 supplies a technical trust envelope and transport safeguards, not a global connector registry. Partner configuration centrally restricts origins, authentication, operation scope, trust state, and limits; owner adapters still own external identifiers, profile mapping, and business reconciliation. Arbitrary caller-supplied URLs and untrusted FHIR references are never fetched. No exactly-once external exchange is claimed.

Phase 10 does not mandate NEMSIS or another jurisdiction-specific EMS protocol. NEMSIS describes EMS/pre-hospital care data collection and reporting in the United States; it is not selected as a universal live dispatch contract. An EMS adapter is provider/deployment-specific and optional; manual Transport operation remains supported.

The R4 resource definitions for [Organization](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/organization.html), [Location](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/location.html), [HealthcareService](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/healthcareservice.html), [Practitioner](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/practitioner.html), [PractitionerRole](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/practitionerrole.html), [ServiceRequest](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/servicerequest.html), [Task](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/task.html), [Schedule](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/schedule.html), and [Slot](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/slot.html) are useful source definitions. They do not settle local operational meanings or ownership.

## Port, mapping, and reliability ownership

- The consuming context owns its port and semantic mapping: Directory for service discovery/mCSD, Referral for patient-context and IPS, Capacity for capacity sources, Transfer for reservation, Matching for travel-time estimates, Transport for dispatch, Notifications for delivery, Identity & Access for the identity provider, and Analytics for approved aggregate exports.
- External identifiers are issued by their source. The context that owns the AyuVahak concept owns its association to the source identifier, scoped by issuer and resource type. There is no central identifier-mapping business context.
- Partner-specific retry, duplicate handling, timeout, reconciliation, and completion meanings belong to the context that owns the operation. Shared libraries may implement bounded transport retries only when safe; they do not decide whether a business action succeeded.
- Adapter-owned configuration names its partner contract and references deployment-managed secrets. There is no central connector registry that owns every country's mappings.
- Terminology services may provide lookup and translation infrastructure. The consuming domain owns meaning, accepted local mappings, and the rule that unmapped codes remain unknown.
- FHIR validation checks wire/profile conformance at the adapter. It does not prove clinical correctness, source freshness, authority, patient match, or AyuVahak authorization.

## Exchange and failure rules

- Keep source identity, provenance, observation/event time, received time, and partner correlation when available.
- Do not treat transport acknowledgement as Acceptance, Reservation, Arrival, or Handover.
- A remote action is pending until its responsible authority confirms it. Duplicate, delayed, out-of-order, and unavailable exchanges are expected conditions.
- Keep patient context purpose-limited. Do not archive every external FHIR resource, IPS document, or attachment. See [data ownership](data-ownership.md).
- DHIS2 exports are governed, minimized aggregates by default; no patient-level case feed or synchronous care-transaction dependency is part of the baseline.
- If an external system already owns the same coordination lifecycle, AyuVahak may be omitted for that deployment.
- Public/shared healthcare systems are not test targets. Later tests use local containers, mocks, deterministic fixtures, or controlled disposable infrastructure.
- When a standard or partner profile does not carry a required coordination fact, define a versioned extension or explicit partner contract; do not overload an unrelated field and imply shared semantics.
