# Standards and Interoperability Strategy

**Verification date:** 2026-10-08. Publication status and implementation-library compatibility are version-sensitive and must be rechecked for a concrete deployment.

## Architectural decision

Standards belong at integration seams. AyuVahak's internal domain language remains Referral, Care Requirement, Capacity Observation, Clinical Suitability Assessment, Operational Readiness Assessment, Acceptance, Reservation, Transfer, Transport, Arrival, and Handover. A domain context owns the port for an external need, the meaning of the exchanged fact or action, partner mapping, and business reconciliation. Its adapter translates the partner contract. Shared interoperability capabilities provide technical support; they do not route every interaction through a universal business gateway. See [bounded contexts](bounded-contexts.md) and [ADR 0010](../adr/0010-domain-owned-integration-ports.md).

Use HL7 FHIR R4 (4.0.1) as the initial interoperability baseline. Keep later R4B (4.3.0) and R5 (5.0.0) support in separately versioned boundary adapters. On the verification date, R5 was the latest final FHIR release shown in HL7's publication directory; R6 6.0.0-ballot5 and a development snapshot were prerelease work, not a final published release. This is a project compatibility choice, not a claim that R4 is the newest standard. Confirm each partner's profiles, terminology, workflow, security, and supported FHIR version before exchange.

## Verified standard and product boundaries

“Standard fact” describes what the published specification or product supports. “AyuVahak decision” describes how this platform chooses to use it. A fact does not itself require the decision.

| Area | Standard or product fact verified | AyuVahak decision and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FHIR releases | HL7 lists R4 4.0.1, R4B 4.3.0, and R5 5.0.0 as published releases; the R6 ballot and snapshot are prerelease. R4 is a mixed Normative/STU release and is superseded by R5 in the publication sequence. | R4 is the first exchange baseline for ecosystem compatibility. Keep version-specific wire models at adapters; do not make FHIR resources the internal domain or storage model. Add R4B/R5 only for a defined partner need and explicit conformance review. |
| FHIR resource building blocks | R4 defines Organization, Location, HealthcareService, Practitioner, and PractitionerRole for organizational, geographic, service, individual, and role-related information. ServiceRequest can represent a referral or transfer-of-care request in appropriate workflows. Task can track execution of a task. Schedule and Slot address appointment scheduling availability. | These resources may be boundary representations when their semantics and partner profiles fit. They do not define AyuVahak's complete Referral, Transfer, Acceptance, Reservation, or Handover lifecycle. A resource's similar name does not make it an AyuVahak aggregate. |
| Operational capacity | FHIR Schedule/Slot describe appointment availability. The reviewed R4 resource set does not provide a universal live operational model for staffed beds, ICU capability, equipment, readiness, or reservation guarantees. | Capacity stays an attributed, freshness-aware domain observation. Missing data is unknown, never false/zero or available by default. An adapter must use a documented local profile or partner contract for operational capacity. |

It supports RESTful discovery and care-service resource exchange, including organization, location, healthcare-service, practitioner/role, and endpoint patterns. The guide says its used features are unchanged in R4B and provides R4 and R4B packages. | mCSD is a useful Directory adapter contract where a deployment supports it. Directory owns partner mapping and its attributed projection. mCSD does not establish live capacity, readiness, Acceptance, Reservation, or the transfer lifecycle. Registry writes are excluded by default absent explicit deployment authority. |

Validation/extraction is performed by the Referral-owned adapter and mapped into the bounded coordination context. |
| SMART App Launch | SMART App Launch 2.2.0 (STU 2.2, current published version verified) is an HL7 FHIR R4 implementation guide based on OAuth 2.0 patterns for app launch, authorization, authentication, and scopes; the guide describes compatibility with FHIR DSTU2 and later. | Use it where a partner's FHIR application-launch contract supports it. It does not supply AyuVahak's complete workforce identity, local role grants, or case/action authorization. Identity & Access still evaluates local policy. |

WHO SMART may inform future domain content or interoperability artifacts where locally adopted; it does not authorize clinical routing rules or make them universal. Safety-critical AyuVahak matching remains deterministic and governed by local policy. |
| OpenHIE / OpenHIM | The reviewed OpenHIE Architecture Specification 4.0 describes the Interoperability Layer (IOL) as securing and orchestrating exchange and says a workflow engine is outside the IOL component. The deployment must name one durable owner for referral/transfer workflow. OpenHIM's technical ability to orchestrate does not mean it must own AyuVahak's lifecycle; conversely, AyuVahak is optional where an existing mediator/application already owns that lifecycle. |
| DHIS2 | Current DHIS2 documentation describes aggregate and individual-level Tracker data models, distinct collection/analytics paths, and patterns for deriving aggregate values from Tracker data. The Tracker API documentation identifies tracked entities, enrollments, events, and relationships. | DHIS2 may receive approved aggregate coordination indicators downstream. It is not categorically aggregate-only, but the AyuVahak baseline does not use it as the operational referral/transfer store and does not put it in a synchronous care transaction. If a deployment already uses Tracker for this workflow, select one owner and avoid split-brain capture. |
| LMIS / OpenLMIS | OpenLMIS Reference Distribution 3.20.1 was a stable release published 2026-10-05; its documentation includes Stock Management, Requisition, and Fulfillment components. A future care-related stock signal needs a scoped decision and cannot be substituted for service Capacity or Readiness. |

HAPI distinguishes plain server/parser support from a JPA server with its own persistence model. | HAPI may be evaluated for boundary parsing/client support when a later-phase FHIR adapter is selected; it is not a Phase 1 dependency or connector. Pin and verify the exact selected version, profiles, license, and security support before that adapter is implemented. Do not adopt HAPI JPA as a parallel clinical store or as AyuVahak's persistence model. |

The R4 resource definitions for [Organization](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/organization.html), [Location](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/location.html), [HealthcareService](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/healthcareservice.html), [Practitioner](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/practitioner.html), [PractitionerRole](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/practitionerrole.html), [ServiceRequest](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/servicerequest.html), [Task](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/task.html), [Schedule](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/schedule.html), and [Slot](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/slot.html) are useful source definitions. They do not settle local operational meanings or ownership.

## Port, mapping, and reliability ownership

- The consuming context owns its port and semantic mapping: Directory for service discovery/mCSD, Referral for patient-context and IPS, Capacity for capacity sources, Transfer for reservation, Matching for travel-time estimates, Transport for dispatch, Notifications for delivery, Identity & Access for the identity provider, and Analytics for approved aggregate exports.
- External identifiers are issued by their source. The context that owns the AyuVahak concept owns its association to the source identifier, scoped by issuer and resource type. There is no central identifier-mapping business context.
- Partner-specific retry, duplicate handling, timeout, reconciliation, and completion meanings belong to the context that owns the operation. Shared libraries may implement bounded transport retries only when safe; they do not decide whether a business action succeeded.
- Adapter-owned configuration names its partner contract and references deployment-managed secrets. There is no central connector registry that owns every country's mappings.
- Terminology services may provide lookup and translation infrastructure. The consuming domain owns meaning, accepted local mappings, and the rule that unmapped codes remain unknown.
- FHIR validation checks wire conformance at the adapter. It does not prove clinical correctness, source freshness, or AyuVahak authorization.

## Exchange and failure rules

- Keep source identity, provenance, observation/event time, received time, and partner correlation when available.
- Do not treat transport acknowledgement as Acceptance, Reservation, Arrival, or Handover.
- A remote action is pending until its responsible authority confirms it. Duplicate, delayed, out-of-order, and unavailable exchanges are expected conditions.
- Keep patient context purpose-limited. Do not archive every external FHIR resource, IPS document, or attachment. See [data ownership](data-ownership.md).
- DHIS2 exports are governed, minimized aggregates by default; no patient-level case feed or synchronous care-transaction dependency is part of the baseline.
- If an external system already owns the same coordination lifecycle, AyuVahak may be omitted for that deployment.
- Public/shared healthcare systems are not test targets. Later tests use local containers, mocks, deterministic fixtures, or controlled disposable infrastructure.
- When a standard or partner profile does not carry a required coordination fact, define a versioned extension or explicit partner contract; do not overload an unrelated field and imply shared semantics.
