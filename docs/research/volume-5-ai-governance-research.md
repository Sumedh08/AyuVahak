# Volume V Phase 14 AI Governance and Health-AI Research

**Research retrieved:** 2026-10-20
**Scope:** Read-only review of current official primary guidance relevant to the design of optional, non-authoritative Phase 14 intelligence. No model/API, healthcare system, or external service was contacted.
**Use:** Design input, not legal advice, certification, or a claim of conformance.

## Executive design consequences

1. Treat NIST AI RMF 1.0 as the currently published baseline identified by NIST on the retrieval date, but record that NIST says its revision is in progress. Pin the edition used by each approved use case and re-check official status before implementation or deployment.
2. Require a lifecycle risk record for every use case: intended purpose and affected actors; limits; data and component provenance; contextual harms; evaluation design and evidence; human oversight; monitoring; incident handling; and an accountable go/no-go decision.
3. Keep model output advisory and outside AyuVahak owner commands. Use deterministic owner facts and deterministic policy as the source of truth. Model unavailability, invalid output, or uncertain output must abstain or fall back to the existing deterministic/no-model workflow.
4. Default intelligence inputs to authorized, bounded, non-clinical coordination facts, aggregate Analytics facts, or documentation. Exclude PatientReference, IPS, names, diagnoses, narrative clinical content, credentials, and secrets. Do not send health payloads to an external provider for acceptance testing.
5. Require use-case-specific evaluation against a no-intelligence and deterministic baseline, using synthetic or approved non-sensitive fixtures. Define task-specific factuality, omissions, harmful-output, abstention, latency, and user-oversight measures as appropriate. Do not use a generic accuracy or confidence score as a safety claim.
6. Require a meaningful, visible human decision point for any advisory output that could affect coordination choices; record the advisory provenance and allow rejection without obstructing the deterministic workflow. Use shadow/advisory evaluation before any material decision-support use.
7. Treat externally supplied text as untrusted model input. Prompts, RAG, or fine-tuning are not security boundaries. Apply least privilege and deterministic input/output validation; initial Phase 14 models receive no tool/action execution, arbitrary URL, SQL, shell, or owner-mutation authority.
8. Evaluate disparity and representativeness in the specific use context and language/population slices that matter. If the evaluation basis or acceptable limits cannot be established, do not claim fairness or enable a consequential use case on that basis.
9. Treat provider/model changes, prompt/template changes, data-distribution shifts, or use-case changes as re-evaluation triggers. Preserve bounded provenance without retaining sensitive prompts/responses by default.
10. Do not infer one jurisdiction's legal classification for every deployment. An intended-purpose change toward clinical decision support or a regulated medical-device function requires a separate deployment-specific regulatory/privacy review before activation.

## Design implications by concern

### Transparency and system/model documentation

NIST AI RMF Map 2.2 calls for documentation of system knowledge limits and how outputs may be used and overseen. Measure outcomes require documented evaluation methods, limitations/generalization conditions, explanation, privacy, fairness, security, and risk measurement. WHO health-AI guidance similarly emphasizes transparency, intelligibility, accountability, and human autonomy.

**Proposed AyuVahak design artifact (a local design choice, not an official mandated “model card” format):** one versioned capability sheet per enabled use case, containing intended/prohibited use, affected users, allowed input classes, source and provider/model/version, template/configuration version, known limits, evaluation corpus and metrics, subgroup/language coverage and gaps, human decision point, abstention/fallback behavior, residual risks, monitoring/incident owner, disablement procedure, retention/training-use terms, and approval/re-evaluation triggers. Keep raw prompts, raw outputs, and sensitive input bodies out of durable provenance by default; use a bounded input fingerprint and reference to the deterministic source facts instead.

### Evaluation, human oversight, and abstention

NIST calls for predeployment and ongoing measurement in conditions like the intended context, documentation of limits, defined roles for human-AI configurations, and evaluation/monitoring of validity, reliability, safety, privacy, security, and fairness. WHO health guidance makes human autonomy, safety, accountability, equity, and understandable use explicit. NIST's generative profile warns about confabulation and automation bias.

**AyuVahak design consequence:** an evaluation contract is required before any use case is enabled. It identifies intended users and decisions supported, prohibited uses, a no-intelligence/deterministic baseline, use-case-specific success and harm metrics, synthetic/approved fixture corpus, acceptance thresholds and known limitations, abstention cases, human review/rejection flow, monitoring signals, and change/retest triggers. A confidence number is not a substitute for calibration evidence. Material decision-support uses stay in shadow/advisory evaluation until an accountable review authorizes narrowly bounded use. Human oversight is meaningful only when the person can understand the advisory status, inspect deterministic source facts, reject it, and continue the normal workflow.

### Fairness and representation

NIST AI RMF Measure 2.11 calls for fairness/bias evaluation and documentation. NIST Playbook guidance emphasizes context-specific metrics and defining actions if disparities exceed governance limits. WHO warns that systems built from narrow populations/settings can underperform in other contexts, including lower-resource languages.

**AyuVahak design consequence:** the evaluation contract declares relevant groups/languages and why they are relevant to that use case, checks coverage/representativeness and outcome/error differences, describes limits of the evidence, and names an accountable threshold/response. No universal “fairness score” or unsupported claim is allowed. If required comparison data or meaningful thresholds are unavailable for a consequential use, defer/disable that use rather than treating absence of evidence as fairness.

### Privacy, provider boundaries, and prompt injection

NIST's generative profile identifies privacy risks from disclosure, inference, and training-data uncertainty, and notes third-party component visibility and security concerns. WHO identifies confidentiality and data governance among health-AI concerns. OWASP LLM01:2025 treats prompt injection as structurally difficult to eliminate, including indirect injection from files/web content, and explicitly notes that RAG/fine-tuning do not fully mitigate it.

**AyuVahak design consequence:** initial model inputs are allowlisted structured coordination fields, aggregate values, or approved documentation only; clinical content is excluded. External providers require an administrator-configured profile for purpose, data classes, endpoint, retention, training use, jurisdiction where required, credentials, and model allowlist; callers cannot select a URL. Provider-independent ports and a fully disabled deployment mode are required. Model output is untrusted data: strict schema/length/enum validation, output sanitization, fixed non-privileged application authority, no model-selected network/SQL/shell/tool invocation, and source-grounded review remain outside the model. Test the injection and privacy boundary against local deterministic fakes only.

### Provenance and change management

NIST AI RMF treats documentation and monitoring as lifecycle practices; its generative profile calls attention to third-party model/data/software component risks. NIST SP 800-218A adapts secure-development practices to AI model/system producers and acquirers.

**AyuVahak design consequence:** store only bounded provenance needed to identify the use case, provider/model revision, template/config/policy version, generation time, input fingerprint and source version, validation/abstention result, and evaluation/governance approval. Do not claim exact reproducibility for nondeterministic models. Re-run the use-case evaluation after provider/model/template/config changes, material data-distribution change, incidents, or intended-purpose change. Model updates cannot silently become active.

## Legal and regulatory boundary

WHO's 2023 regulatory publication explicitly describes itself as a general, non-exclusive list of considerations rather than a regulation or policy. NIST AI RMF also says applicable legal and regulatory requirements depend on context. The initial Phase 14 boundary excludes clinical diagnosis, treatment recommendations, clinical triage, patient risk scoring, and autonomous coordination authority. That boundary informs design but **does not classify the product for every jurisdiction**. Before a deployment enables a capability whose intended purpose may be clinical decision support, medical-device software, or another regulated use, require a documented jurisdiction-specific classification and legal/privacy review. Do not make one jurisdiction's legal requirements universal product defaults.
