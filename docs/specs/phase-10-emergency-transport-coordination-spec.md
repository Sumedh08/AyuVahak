# Phase 10 specification — Emergency Transport Coordination

**Status:** specification frozen. This file is the canonical Phase 10 behavior contract; implementation and acceptance status are recorded in `docs/reviews/phase-10-acceptance.md`.

**Volume:** III.

**Depends on:** frozen Volume I–II kernel and contexts; Phase 9 exchange trust boundary where a configured provider is used. Manual coordination must work without Phase 9 external connectivity.
**Architecture pointers:** [Volume III architecture](../architecture/volume-3-interoperability-security-transport.md), [context contracts](../architecture/volume-3-context-contracts.md), [state machines](../architecture/volume-3-state-machines.md), [threat model](../architecture/volume-3-threat-model.md), [Transfer state machines](../architecture/volume-2-state-machines.md), ADR 0010/0011/0012/0013/0017 and Volume III ADRs.

## Outcome and scope

Add a Transport business bounded context for coordinating the movement needed by a Transfer. It owns transport requests, provider assignment references, attributed operational status observations, ETA observations, cancellation/reconciliation, movement evidence, and transport exceptions. It does not own clinical care, Transfer acceptance, Capacity reservations, destination selection, vehicle fleet inventory, or provider dispatch algorithms.

Transport is optional per deployment and per Transfer. A Transfer can use no managed Transport, an external EMS/transport provider, facility-owned transport, or authorized manual movement recording. No external provider is required for core Transfer operation.

## Explicit non-goals

No ambulance fleet ERP, computer-aided dispatch replacement, automatic call taking, fleet GPS surveillance, route optimization, vehicle maintenance, crew HR/rostering, payroll, provider clinical record, public EMS database, provider-specific domain model, autonomous dispatch, clinical triage/vehicle selection, Google Maps/Mapbox requirement, broker, or required continuous location feed. NEMSIS and IHE EMS data profiles are not required Phase 10 protocols; NEMSIS is a jurisdiction-specific EMS data/reporting standard, not a universal live-dispatch contract.

## Context ownership

- `Transfer` owns the care-transition Journey, destination attempt, receiving clinical suitability/readiness assessments, Acceptance, Capacity reservation relationship, departure/IN_TRANSIT fact, Transfer Arrival, Handover, Completion, interruption and rerouting authority.
- `Transport` owns its own request, assignment reference, expected pickup/ETA observations, operational status history, provider cancellation/reconciliation and transport completion/interruption.
- The provider remains the source of truth for vehicle availability/inventory, crew roster, dispatch algorithm and its assignment record. AyuVahak stores only a provider-scoped reference and the minimal status evidence needed to coordinate.
- Directory references provider organizations/locations only where those facts are already configured; Transport does not build a second provider directory.
- Transport cannot read or write Transfer tables. Transfer cannot read or write Transport tables. Cross-context effects use owner contracts and named minimal events.
- Assignment is not Acceptance; provider dispatch is not Transfer departure; transport arrival is not Transfer Handover.

## Request, assignment, and manual workflow

The initiating human must be authorized by Transfer for the requested Transfer action. Transfer passes an explicit, validated actor/execution context and its dedicated service identity to Transport. Transport independently validates that service identity's narrowly scoped request grant and queries Transfer's owner contract `CheckTransportRequestEligibility` before creating anything. Transfer returns `ELIGIBLE` only when transport is required, the current Transfer is in the permitted pre-departure phase, its active destination has the required receiving Acceptance and confirmed Capacity reservation, and the supplied origin/destination references exactly match Transfer-owned state. A denied/ineligible, unavailable, or ambiguous result creates no request. Transport does not infer authority from a Transfer ID. Direct manual Transport actions require the corresponding Transport functional authority and still require an eligible linked Transfer. The request passes only the Transfer reference, origin/destination references, approved pickup window/priority, specifically allowlisted operational constraints, reason/purpose, correlation, and idempotency identity. It does not include PatientReference, diagnosis, or a clinical narrative. If no provider or connectivity exists, the outcome is `MANUAL_COORDINATION_REQUIRED`, `UNFULFILLED`, or another explicit non-success; the Transfer remains in its actual state.

An integrated provider path uses a Transport-owned dispatch port and Phase 9 trust profile. The provider adapter maps its own DTO/status codes into Transport observations. A provider acknowledgement or assignment does not create Transfer movement. A manual path records the human operator, organization/facility context, authority, observed time, recorded time, evidence source/method, reason, and correlation; it uses the same transition policy and audit.

At most one active request exists for a Transfer at a time. A request may be replaced only after the preceding request is closed, cancelled/reconciled, or deliberately transitioned under an explicit safe workflow. Historical attempts and acceptance/assignment facts are never deleted to make a new request appear first.

## Functional authorities and security

Use functional authorities, locally mapped to job roles/policies: `TransportRequestAuthority`, `TransportDispatchCoordinationAuthority`, `TransportStatusAuthority`, `TransportCancellationAuthority`, `TransportDestinationChangeAuthority`, and Transfer's `TransferRerouteAuthority`. These names express authority functions, not mandatory roles or Java interfaces. An identity may hold only the scoped grants authorized by deployment policy. The Transfer-to-Transport machine identity has only the specific request/cancellation/destination-change operations it needs at explicitly granted scopes (normally the origin-side organization/facility scope); Transport checks that scope against Transfer-owned facts through `CheckTransportRequestEligibility` and `CheckTransportDestinationChangeEligibility`. It does not inherit a human's Transfer grants or obtain authority merely from a Transfer identifier. Human reroute actions are authorized by Transfer; Transport separately authorizes the service's operation and records the initiating actor as attribution only. Direct manual Transport actions require their own Transport authority. Provider machine identity is distinct from the AyuVahak service identity and from the external OAuth client credential. Every mutation and accepted inbound status is attributed, authorized, idempotent, and audited.

Cross-organization commands require valid local grants and explicit execution context. The partner identity authenticates the source but does not grant status authority for all Transfers. The owner checks the partner’s configured operation and scope. Duplicate external IDs with different content, invalid signatures, wrong scope, revoked provider, and stale/out-of-order status fail closed or enter explicit reconciliation.

## Transfer coordination contract

- **Request:** Transfer → Transport, synchronous local command. Transport returns its internal request reference and current coordination outcome. If a provider send is asynchronous, return pending/unavailable rather than claiming assigned.
- **Request eligibility:** Before accepting the request, Transport calls Transfer-owned `CheckTransportRequestEligibility` using the scoped service identity. Transfer returns only `ELIGIBLE`, `INELIGIBLE` with a bounded reason, or `UNAVAILABLE/AMBIGUOUS`; it does not expose a private repository or clinical data. The query verifies the active Transfer relationship, permitted pre-departure state, transport requirement, current destination, required Acceptance/reservation, and exact origin/destination match. Transport fails closed if the query is unavailable or ambiguous.
- **Cancel:** Transfer first authorizes its human's Transfer action and invokes Transport with a dedicated scoped service identity; alternatively an actor with `TransportCancellationAuthority` may invoke Transport directly. Transport independently validates the caller grant/scope and creates/replays a durable `TransportCancellationOperation` using a stable operation ID. Pending/unknown outcome blocks replacement. A late assignment is retained and does not clear cancellation intent; repeat cancellation uses the same identity only when safe under the provider contract.
- **Cancellation outcome:** Transport exposes `GetTransportCancellationOutcome` only to its linked Transfer service identity and emits `transport.cancellation-resolved.v1` when an outcome is resolved. The event has a named Transfer consumer, minimal linked references/outcome/source/time/correlation/causation, and no patient data or raw provider body. Transfer deduplicates, validates current linkage, and updates only its own state. `CANCELLED_BEFORE_MOVEMENT` permits Transfer to consider its own next action after revalidation; `CANCEL_REJECTED_ACTIVE` or `MOVEMENT_ALREADY_STARTED` does not permit replacement. Unknown/reconciliation-required remains blocking.
- **Departure:** Transport emits `transport.departure-confirmed.v1` only when validated evidence shows patient onboard and movement away from origin, or a human records a validated explicit departure. Transfer verifies selected active Transport/Transfer linkage, authorization/policy, chronology and duplicate state before recording its own `IN_TRANSIT` fact. Assignment, ETA, en-route-to-origin, and at-origin do not qualify.
- **Arrival:** Transport emits `transport.arrival-reported.v1` for source evidence that transport reached destination. Transfer uses its owner command to record Transfer Arrival only if actor/source scope, active destination and chronology are valid. Arrival does not imply Handover or Completion.
- **Interruption/unfulfilled:** Transport emits `transport.interrupted.v1` or `transport.unfulfilled.v1` only with a named Transfer consumer/purpose. Transfer records an exception or needs-reconciliation outcome; it does not automatically choose another destination or reroute.
- **Pre-movement destination change:** Transfer follows its existing attempt/reroute rules and safe cancellation/replacement semantics; a replacement request must pass `CheckTransportRequestEligibility` for the new active destination.
- **Post-movement destination change:** Transfer owns the reroute decision. An actor with `TransferRerouteAuthority` supplies a reason and current attributed movement evidence. Transfer obtains a fresh applicable Matching result, receiving decisions required by policy, and a confirmed Capacity reservation for the proposed destination; it durably records an immutable pending reroute intent and audit while keeping the current destination active. Transfer then invokes Transport with a stable operation ID and scoped service identity. Transport independently checks `TransportDestinationChangeAuthority` and calls `CheckTransportDestinationChangeEligibility`, which confirms the exact linked request and pending intent without returning patient context. A provider acknowledgement or separately authorized manual crew/provider acknowledgement is required; a local request submission is not acknowledgement. Transport owns `TransportDestinationChangeOperation` and publishes `transport.destination-change-resolved.v1` for a concrete `ACCEPTED`/`REJECTED` result. Transfer alone consumes, deduplicates, and revalidates it before changing its active destination. On `ACCEPTED`, Transfer first commits the new active destination and an old-hold release intent, then calls Capacity's existing `ReleaseCoordinationReservation`/`GetReservationOutcome` with a separate stable operation ID. The new destination remains active if old release is unknown; expose a capacity-cleanup exception, query/retry the same operation, and block another reroute until `RELEASED` or `EXPIRED` is confirmed. Do not claim the old hold released and do not roll back an accepted physical reroute. On `REJECTED`, the old destination stays active and Transfer releases the proposed hold with its own stable operation ID; unknown release similarly blocks another reroute. Unknown provider outcomes retain both destination/reservation facts and block competing lifecycle changes pending scoped outcome recovery/manual reconciliation. If movement reaches the old destination or the new evidence/reservation is no longer valid before the acknowledgement is applied, do not claim the reroute succeeded; create a durable coordination exception and resolve with an authorized operator. There is no generic destination patch or automatic route choice.
- **Destination-change outcome:** Transport exposes `GetTransportDestinationChangeOutcome` only to the linked Transfer service identity. It emits `transport.destination-change-resolved.v1` with a minimal accepted/rejected outcome only after a concrete provider or authorized manual acknowledgement. Transfer validates linkage, operation identity, current movement, recipient decisions, and reservation before applying its own destination change; an unknown or no-longer-applicable result remains an explicit reconciliation exception.

All events are at-least-once, minimal, versioned using the frozen event convention, and include event/aggregate sequence as needed, actor/service or partner source, source/received/recorded times, correlation and causation. Consumers deduplicate locally. No PatientReference, clinical payload, credentials, arbitrary provider JSON, or continuous location history is included. No global event ordering or exactly-once claim is added. Cancellation and destination-change resolution events are emitted only when the owner has a concrete outcome; a pending or unknown operation is recovered through the owner query and remains blocking.

## State machine

Use the [Transport state machine](../architecture/volume-3-state-machines.md) as normative. The minimal lifecycle is:

```text
REQUESTED → AWAITING_ASSIGNMENT → ASSIGNED → EN_ROUTE_TO_ORIGIN
→ AT_ORIGIN → PATIENT_ONBOARD → EN_ROUTE_TO_DESTINATION
→ ARRIVED_DESTINATION → COMPLETED
```

From states before patient movement, explicit outcomes may be `CANCELLED` or `UNFULFILLED`; after assignment/movement, failure uses `INTERRUPTED` with a reason/evidence and reconciliation. Cancellation intent is a separate `TransportCancellationOperation`, not a request-state replacement. A post-movement destination change is a separate `TransportDestinationChangeOperation` correlated to a Transfer-owned pending reroute intent. The current destination changes only after a concrete provider/manual acknowledgement and a successful Transfer-side revalidation/commit. Unknown, contradictory, rejected, or stale outcomes never imply the destination changed. `CANCELLED` is recorded only after a confirmed pre-movement outcome. No implicit rollback or deletion of observations. Terminal states remain terminal. A correction or out-of-order source fact is appended and reconciled. ETA changes are observations, not state transitions.

## Acceptance / TDD seams

All examples use synthetic values such as `SYN-TRANSFER-001`, `SYN-FACILITY-A`, and `SYN-TRANSPORT-PROVIDER-1`. Tests must use a local EMS mock server or deterministic fixtures, local disposable PostgreSQL, synthetic local certificates/tokens, and test doubles. No live ambulance, EMS, routing, map, public safety, or government endpoint is a test target.

- Manual request, manual assignment/status, and manual movement complete without an external provider.
- Provider request response lost after provider accepts; recover via the same operation identity/provider idempotency or mark unknown and reconcile; no duplicate dispatch.
- Duplicate same message is idempotent; same provider event ID with a different fingerprint is rejected/audited; stale sequence and future source time follow bounded clock-skew policy.
- Out-of-order `ASSIGNED`, `PATIENT_ONBOARD`, `EN_ROUTE_TO_ORIGIN`; impossible transition does not regress or advance state and is visible for reconciliation.
- Assignment and cancellation race; the cancellation operation stays pending/unknown while a late assignment observation is recorded. Verify a safe same-operation retry or explicit reconciliation, no replacement while unresolved, a named resolution event/query to Transfer, and no reopening after confirmed cancellation. If movement began, report `MOVEMENT_ALREADY_STARTED`; do not claim cancellation stopped it.
- Transport request is rejected without creation when Transfer eligibility says ineligible, unavailable, or ambiguous; it succeeds only for exact current origin/destination references and a permitted Transfer state.
- Transfer cancellation versus Transport assignment; before movement it requests cancellation; during movement it records the actual movement and uses interruption/coordination rules.
- Duplicate arrival; Transport arrival before Transfer digital state; Transfer applies its own Arrival without inventing departure, Acceptance, or reservation.
- Handover and Completion remain Transfer-owned and require their own authorities/facts; ambulance arrival cannot cause them.
- Destination reroute before/after boarding; post-movement reroute records a Transfer-owned pending intent, obtains fresh Matching/receiver decisions and a confirmed new reservation, and changes the active destination only after the linked provider/manual acceptance is received and Transfer revalidates. Verify no old-reservation release occurs before that commit; afterward the old hold is released with a distinct stable Capacity operation ID. Exercise same-operation transport retry, provider rejection, lost/unknown transport response, lost/unknown Capacity-release response with same-ID recovery, unavailable/conflicting Capacity release, destination evidence changing while pending, old-destination arrival while pending, duplicate outcome, unauthorized actor/service, and audit failure. Confirm accepted destination remains active during old-hold cleanup, unknown release remains visible and blocks another reroute, rejection keeps the old destination and releases the proposed hold, and neither path claims release before `RELEASED`/`EXPIRED`. Verify no private table reads and no patient data in the operation/event.
- Provider outage, revocation during retry, no provider, no network; manual/exception path remains visible and Transfer facts remain unchanged.
- Privacy tests prove PatientReference, diagnoses, clinical summary, tokens, and exact location history are absent from Transport persistence, events, audit, logs, metrics, traces, and Problem Details.
- Architecture tests prohibit Transfer↔Transport private repository access, transport provider DTOs in domain, direct network calls from domains, and unauthorized event dependencies.
- Verify authorization scope, audit failure fail-closed behavior where required, owner-local idempotency, event outbox atomicity/recovery, and bounded telemetry.

Acceptance requires both integrated transport-provider and manual workflows to demonstrate truthful state with event duplicates, delays, or failure. Provider unavailability must never imply that a patient moved or that Transfer completed.
