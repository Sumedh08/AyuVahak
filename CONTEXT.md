# AyuVahak Domain Context

AyuVahak is an optional coordination system for health systems whose organizations need a shared, accountable cross-facility workflow. Its unit of work is a purpose-limited coordination case, not a patient's lifetime clinical record. If an existing system already owns the required referral and transfer workflow, a deployment may integrate with that system or omit AyuVahak.

## The coordination problem

A referring care team identifies a need for care beyond its current facility. The team states the Care Requirement and remains responsible for clinical judgement. AyuVahak helps discover possible destinations, assess attributed operational evidence, request a receiving facility's response, coordinate a capacity commitment and patient movement, and reconcile arrival and handover.

These are related but distinct decisions and facts. Receiving-side authority is defined by function and mapped by local policy, not by a universal job title:

- The Referral describes the care need and coordination request.
- A Destination Recommendation describes what the platform could justify from available evidence at a point in time.
- A Clinical Suitability Assessment and an Operational Readiness Assessment are separately attributable receiving-side facts. An Acceptance Authority records the facility's willingness only after both are positive. Local policy may map each function to a clinician, transfer-center nurse, bed manager, specialty registrar, referral desk, command center, team, or governed process; one person may perform multiple functions only when explicitly authorized.
- Reservation records a separate operational commitment.
- Transfer records the patient movement and its completion. Referral status summarizes requests and linked Transfer attempts; Transfer remains authoritative for reservation and movement details.
- Transport records an arrangement for moving the patient; dispatch authority remains with the transport organization.
- Arrival and Handover are separate facts.

A recommendation cannot promise future availability. Acceptance cannot stand in for a reservation. A reservation cannot stand in for admission. Arrival cannot stand in for clinical handover.

## Authority is category-specific

There is no universal “healthcare source of truth.” A clinical system may be authoritative for a patient's record, a facility registry for facility identity, the locally designated receiving-side authority for a current capacity observation, an identity provider for authentication, and AyuVahak for the coordination history it records when selected as workflow owner. Copies, caches, and exchanged summaries retain their source and observation time; they do not silently become authoritative.

AyuVahak owns the coordination state necessary to run and audit its workflow. It avoids owning records already authoritative elsewhere. A coordination view can be wrong or stale even when its source was authoritative when it supplied the information.

## Decision rights and trust

Clinical teams state care needs and retain clinical decision rights. A locally mapped Clinical Suitability Authority assesses whether the destination can meet the case requirement. A separately mapped Operational Readiness Authority assesses current operational ability. An Acceptance Authority records facility willingness after both assessments are positive. Each is a decision function, not a universal job title; local policy maps it to roles, teams, or an explicitly governed process. Dispatch authorities assign transport. Administrators govern local participation and access. AyuVahak may surface evidence, exclusions, freshness, and a deterministic explanation; it does not make clinical triage decisions or silently commit a destination.

Authentication identity and patient identity are different concepts. An external identity provider authenticates human and separately scoped machine actors. AyuVahak validates API access tokens and independently evaluates its own scoped grants against an explicit, server-validated organization and optional facility context, action, target, and purpose. A caller-selected organization is not trusted until validated. Local roles map to functional authorities by deployment policy; token claims do not substitute for application authorization. Information supplied by a trusted system is still checked for provenance, scope, and freshness. Least privilege applies to patient context, operational capacity, analytics, and technical operations independently.

Safety- and access-relevant actions are attributable through a restricted audit record. A required audit record for a protected mutation is committed with that mutation; failure rolls back both. Sensitive reads and non-mutating security outcomes use a separate durable audit path with explicit fail-closed behavior. This chronology is not the clinical record, ordinary operational logs, or provenance of every datum.

## System boundary and evolution

AyuVahak begins when an authorized referral is created and its minimum necessary context is available in a deployment that has selected AyuVahak as the workflow owner. Its coordination responsibility ends when the case is completed through arrival and handover, or explicitly cancelled or marked unfulfilled with the outcome recorded. Downstream clinical care remains in the receiving clinical system.

The product can operate with human-maintained directories and capacity observations before mature interoperability is available. Each domain responsibility owns the meaning of the external facts it consumes; shared interoperability capabilities can assist adapters without becoming the universal gateway or owner of a business workflow. The product should preserve provenance and expose uncertainty in every environment. Over time, standardized exchanges and federation can reduce manual work, but they do not change the domain's authority rules or turn AyuVahak into an EHR, health-information exchange, dispatch platform, or public-health transaction system.

## Platform kernel context

The approved [Phase 2 platform-kernel architecture](docs/architecture/phase-2-platform-kernel.md) supplies common trust and reliability capabilities before healthcare workflows are implemented. Identity & Access and Audit are platform bounded contexts; provenance, event contracts, outbox delivery, and duplicate protection are shared capabilities or locally owned mechanisms, not a central store of business truth. Domain events remain producer-owned, at-least-once delivery is assumed, and causal order is promised only per aggregate where required. Command retry protection and consumer duplicate protection have separate owners. Phase 2 is implemented and frozen; business-context implementation remains separately governed by its phase gates.

## Volume II core care coordination

Phases 3–7 add five business contexts inside the existing modular monolith, in order: Directory, Capacity, Referral, Matching, then Transfer. The [Volume II architecture](docs/architecture/volume-2-core-care-coordination.md) and its [cross-context contracts](docs/architecture/volume-2-context-contracts.md) define their ownership and allowed collaboration. Contexts call one another through application contracts or justified internal events; the shared PostgreSQL deployment does not make another context's schema or repository a public API.

Directory holds a source-attributed coordination projection for organizations, facilities, locations, services and stable capability assertions. Capacity owns time-bounded source evidence and its policy-derived current/readiness projection; it may also own an AyuVahak coordination hold in Phase 7. The external authority remains authoritative for its underlying facility, bed, workforce or source facts. Capacity evaluates a required set of CapacityDimensions but does not infer those dimensions from CareCapabilities. Matching owns the versioned policy that maps each required CareCapability to operational dimensions; a missing mapping blocks a positive recommendation. Unknown, stale, expired and conflicted evidence is explicit, never zero or positive.

Referral stores an immutable submitted revision of the originating team's structured Care Requirement and an opaque issuer-scoped PatientReference. The Volume II default stores no demographics, diagnosis, clinical narrative, attachment, or full Patient Summary. Clinical detail remains in the originating/receiving clinical systems and is exchanged directly or out of band under local authority. Matching evaluates only explicit requirements and source-attributed Directory/Capacity evidence; it produces an immutable, versioned advisory snapshot. It does not make clinical or destination decisions. Transfer owns sequential receiving request attempts and transfer chronology; Capacity owns reservation state. Acceptance, Reservation, Transfer, Transport, Arrival, and Handover remain distinct.

Receiving-side Clinical Suitability, Operational Readiness, and Acceptance are policy functions. A deployment maps its local staff, team, or governed process to each function; no universal job title is assumed. Transfer acceptance requires separate positive receiving assessments and does not imply a Capacity reservation. A reservation is a coordination hold, not a physical bed guarantee or admission. A later change may make it `AT_RISK`; the system records the mismatch and requires reconciliation.

An event may precede its executable consumer only when the consumer, purpose, ownership, payload/privacy boundary, and delivery behavior are frozen in the same approved Volume design. `referral.cancelled.v1` meets that rule: Phase 5 owns the fact and the Phase 7 Transfer consumer is now registered. Cancellation rows created before the Transfer consumer existed remain durably marked `WAITING_FOR_CONSUMER`, without retry, failure classification, or consumer receipt; they are not automatically replayed because no Transfer journey could have existed before Phase 7. Newly created cancellation events use the registered handler. Capacity reservation expiry, risk, and evidence-recovery events are emitted by Phase 7's Capacity reservation owner for active holds. A delayed recovery event restores Transfer's referenced Capacity outcome without rewinding later Transfer progress; a Transfer still in `COORDINATION_EXCEPTION` requires receiving-authority reconfirmation before returning to `READY`. Other hypothetical future events are not emitted. Queries and immediate commands remain synchronous application contracts. The Phase 2 outbox remains internal, at-least-once, and does not become a partner integration gateway. Volume II includes no standards connector, dispatch, notifications, analytics, UI, AI, broker, or separate microservice.

## Volume III design context

Volume III is designed as three bounded extensions around the frozen core; its implementation has not started. Phase 8 places FHIR R4, IPS, and mCSD representations in Directory-, Referral-, or Capacity-owned adapters. The source remains authoritative for its underlying facts, Directory owns its coordination projection, and Referral owns patient-context disclosure. Full external resources and IPS documents are transient by default. The FHIR standard does not establish AyuVahak authority, freshness, patient match, or permission.

Phase 9 provides a shared technical partner-trust envelope and bounded exchange transport. It is not a universal gateway or semantic mapping owner. Partner endpoints are administrator-controlled; no caller-supplied URL or untrusted FHIR reference can trigger a fetch. Each business context keeps its external port, identifiers, mapping, retry meaning, and reconciliation. HTTP delivery is not a business outcome, and unknown side effects remain explicitly unknown.

Phase 10 designs Transport as a separate business context. It owns transport requests, assignments as reported references, ETA/status/movement observations, cancellation and reconciliation, and provider/manual destination-change operations. The provider remains authority for fleet/crew/dispatch facts. Transfer remains owner of Acceptance, reservation relationship, care-transition chronology, Arrival, Handover, Completion, destination-change intent, and active destination. A post-movement destination change requires fresh destination evidence, required receiving decisions, a new Capacity reservation, an acknowledged transport instruction, and a Transfer-side state change; old Capacity release is reconciled afterward through Capacity's owner contract. Manual Transport coordination works without EMS integration. Transport arrival is not Transfer Arrival, Handover, or Completion; an explicit attributed movement fact is needed before Transfer records `IN_TRANSIT`.

All external data is treated as unavailable, stale, inaccurate, duplicated, malformed, revoked, or malicious until validated under an owner contract and partner policy. Phase 8–10 tests use only local mocks, synthetic fixtures, local tokens/certificates, and disposable infrastructure. The [Volume III architecture](docs/architecture/volume-3-interoperability-security-transport.md), [contracts](docs/architecture/volume-3-context-contracts.md), [state machines](docs/architecture/volume-3-state-machines.md), and phase specifications are design-only and require separate authorization before implementation.
